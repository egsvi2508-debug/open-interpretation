01 · Context

Where this sonata comes from

AI summary · 6 sources · ✓ checked by 2 musicians

The subtitle is literal. Prokofiev built the Fourth Sonata out of student work: a piano sonata from his St. Petersburg Conservatory years (1908) and an abandoned Symphony in E minor. He rewrote it in 1917, premiered it himself in Petrograd on 17 April 1918, and A. Gutheil published it the same year. The Third Sonata, Op. 28, carries the same subtitle.

The slow movement has the most interesting story. In his diary of April 1917, Prokofiev describes looking for a new Andante, failing to find a lost manuscript, and then remembering the Andante of the E-minor Symphony, which he judged would work just as well on the piano. In 1934 he turned it back into an orchestral piece, the Andante, Op. 29 bis. That orchestral version later becomes one of the best clues for how to play the piano version.

The sonata is dedicated to Maximilian Schmidthof, Prokofiev’s close friend from the Conservatory, who took his own life in 1913. Compared with the brilliant Third and Seventh, the Fourth is inward and dark in its first two movements. Harlow Robinson hears something almost Schumannesque in it. It is played far less often than Nos. 3 and 7, yet some consider it the finest of the nine.

Frank Merrick in 1948 already counted Prokofiev’s sonatas among “true sonatas” in the line of Haydn, Mozart and Beethoven.Merrick, Proceedings of the Royal Musical Association 75 (1948), via Cho 2023, p. 3
